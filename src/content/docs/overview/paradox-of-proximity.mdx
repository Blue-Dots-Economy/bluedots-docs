## What the paradox of proximity means

The **paradox of proximity** is the name Blue Dots gives to a common problem. People who need something often live close to people who offer it, yet they never find each other. A cab app finds a rider and a driver a few kilometres apart in minutes. Most local work, learning, services and schemes have no shared system like that.

Example, Blue Dot (jobs): Kavita, a commerce graduate in Ghaziabad, is urged by her family to move to Delhi or Gurgaon for work. Two kilometres from her house, a manufacturing firm is looking for someone like her. Neither knows the other exists.

Example: A farmer needs a tractor for two days to prepare his field. A custom hiring centre in the next village has a tractor free that week. The farmer doesn't know the centre exists, so he waits until a neighbour can lend him one.

## Most of the district economy is not online

Most work, services and schemes in a district are not listed anywhere online. They appear as posters on walls, poles, autos and hoardings, and people hear about them from friends and relatives. National platforms were built for people who are comfortable online. They don't suit local listings in local languages.

As a result, roughly **80% of the district economy has never appeared on any digital system**.

:::note[What is missing]
In most districts, the services, schemes, training and jobs already exist. What is missing is a way for people to find them. A shared system for listing and finding what already exists costs far less than creating new resources.
:::

## Who is affected most

The problem hits hardest the people who can't rely on who they know. These include women returning to work, persons with disabilities, first-generation graduates without alumni networks, and daily-wage workers. Most people hear about work and services through friends and family. People without those contacts miss out.

A shared system for listing and finding helps everyone in a district, including people with no contacts. For a campaign that collected the needs of persons with disabilities (PwDs), see the Purple Dots section of [The Dots Family](/explore/beyond-livelihoods/).

:::tip[For example, small businesses]
Micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) provide roughly **70% of employment** in India but only about **30% of GDP**. One reason is that they struggle to find nearby workers, customers and services. See [The Economics of Local Discovery](/explore/economics/).
:::

## The size of the opportunity

Helping people find each other in India's **top 100 districts** is a conservatively estimated **₹87,500 crore per year** opportunity. That figure counts only better livelihoods, so the value across all sectors is larger. [The Economics of Local Discovery](/explore/economics/) shows how it is worked out.

## What changes with Blue Dots

The jobs network (Blue) has been piloted in Ghaziabad and Dharwad. The Blue rows below come from [the pilots](/explore/pilots/). Yellow and Green have not been piloted, so their rows describe only the problem and what the software would do.

| Dot | What is measured | Before | With Blue Dots |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blue (jobs) | Jobs visible on platforms | Fewer than 100 listings across the district | 10,000+ from under 10% of MSMEs |
| Blue (jobs) | Cost of collecting local information | ₹500+ per survey interaction | Under ₹10 per interaction (voice AI) |
| Blue (jobs) | Time to find a match | Over 2 weeks | Under 2 days |
| Blue (jobs) | Distance of match | Another city | Within 2 bus stops |
| Blue (jobs) | Placement or conversion rate | About 5% | About 50% |
| Yellow (education) | Finding a tutor for one subject | A student asks relatives or visits coaching centres one by one | Not piloted. Tutors nearby who teach that subject could see the request. |
| Green (farming) | Finding a tractor or a soil test | A farmer asks around the village and often waits | No network yet. A nearby custom hiring centre or soil-testing lab could see the request. |
