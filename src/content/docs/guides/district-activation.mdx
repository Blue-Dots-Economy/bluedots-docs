This guide covers the institutional and operational steps a district government takes to start Blue Dots. Once it runs, people and organisations in the district who need something can find nearby ones who offer it. For the technical steps (setting up Signals, the aggregator portal and the phone and web channels), see [Adaptor Onboarding](/guides/adaptor-onboarding/).

A district does not need to build everything on day one. It needs to do three things. Pick one sector (one domain). Find local organisations on both sides: people who need something, and people who offer it. Then start, and add more as it grows.

:::tip[Decision to activation in under 90 days]
A district can go from decision to activation in **under 90 days**, in three stages of about 4 weeks each: Setup (weeks 1–4), Activate (weeks 5–8) and Drive outcomes (weeks 9–12).
:::

The steps are the same for every domain. The examples below use jobs (the Blue Dot domain), because it is the only domain piloted so far. A *District Livelihoods Playbook* (v1) describes how the jobs pilots did this in practice.

## Choosing the sector to start with

Pick the sector where:

- one department can own it,
- local organisations already work with people on both sides, and
- people clearly fail to find each other locally today.

Jobs is the sector piloted so far. Education (Yellow Dot), disability support (Purple Dot) and tourism (Orange Dot) are defined as domains. See [Domains, Participant Types and Instances](/core-concepts/networks-domains-instances/) for each domain and its status.

## The first 4 weeks of activation

Early activation is about getting enough listings on both sides that people start finding each other without the team arranging each match.

This happens fastest when the team brings in one strong local organisation on each side. For example:

| Domain | Organisations on one side | Organisations on the other side |
|---|---|---|
| Blue (jobs) | ITIs (Industrial Training Institutes) and skilling centres, for young people looking for work | MSME (micro, small and medium enterprise) associations, for employers |
| Yellow (education) | Schools, colleges and education NGOs, for students | Tutors and counsellors |
| Orange (tourism) | Tourism bodies, which check and verify listings | Homestay owners, guides and artists who list what they offer |

Once both sides have enough listings, people begin finding each other without the team pushing each match.

For each group of people, the team chooses how they join. In an **assisted** route, the field team signs them up. In a **self** route, people sign up on their own. The team also picks the intake method for each campaign and place, and this needs no new software. A college batch with existing records is a bulk upload; a member gathering is a QR code campaign. See [The Blue Dot Lifecycle](/core-concepts/blue-dot-lifecycle/#2-choose-the-route-in).

## What the government owns

Local organisations run the sign-ups, and the software does the matching. The government owns three things:

1. **Convening authority:** bringing the right organisations and service providers to the table.
2. **Public backing:** showing that this is a serious programme worth joining.
3. **Sustained attention:** keeping regular reviews going so the work does not stop and start.

:::note[Who holds it]
In practice, this means a **government champion at District Collector level** and a **sponsor at Mission Director or Secretary level**.
:::

In the jobs domain, the **Jobs Facilitation Centre (JFC)** keeps the seeker-side and provider-side organisations working together as more people sign up. Another sector would need a similar coordinating office.

## What success looks like at 90 days

- A live district map with listings from both sides of the chosen sector.
- Local organisations actively signing people up and responding to listings.
- Service providers beginning to offer services on the platform.
- Data on needs and offers that the government and local organisations can use.

:::caution[What made the difference in the pilots]
In Ghaziabad and Dharwad, the fastest activation came where **one senior champion decided to move fast**. The size or complexity of the plan mattered less. See [Pilots](/explore/pilots/).
:::
