A **blue dot**, which the software calls a **signal**, is a short listing that says what a person or organisation needs or offers. The name comes from map apps, which show your own location as a blue dot. The **Signals DPG** (digital public good) is the backend that stores blue dots as schema-typed items and finds matching blue dots from people and organisations nearby, across instances.

Each sector is its own domain, with its own colour of blue dot. Two examples:

- **Blue Dot (jobs).** A young person registers by phone call in their language. A nearby employer with a vacancy sees the profile and sends an interview invite.
- **Yellow Dot (education).** A Class 12 commerce student asks for help with accounts. A tutor who teaches Class 12 commerce and will travel up to 5 km sends a connect request.

The full list of domains is on [Domains, Participant Types & Instances](/core-concepts/networks-domains-instances/#the-domains-today).

## Anatomy of a signal

Every signal is stored as an **item**: a versioned, schema-typed record. The `item_type` is always a schema identifier such as `profile_1.0`, never freeform text. Two instances that exchange signals therefore agree on their structure.

Two fields are generated by the backend during item creation and must **never** be set by clients:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `item_instance_url` | Where the item lives. |
| `item_schema_url` | The schema it conforms to. |

## Who lists a blue dot

Each domain defines its own participant types. Most have two sides:

- Someone who **needs** something: a young person looking for work (Blue), a student looking for a tutor (Yellow), or a person with a disability looking for a device or service (Purple).
- Someone who **offers** it: a local employer, a tutor or counsellor, or an organisation that provides devices and training.

Blue Dots finds compatible blue dots that are close to each other and helps the two sides connect. Some domains have only one side. In the Orange Dot (tourism) domain, homestays, artisans and guides publish listings that visitors browse.

## Consent and contact details

<Aside type="note" title="A blue dot is published by choice">
Every blue dot exists because the person or organisation it describes chose to publish it. It is stored, found and exchanged across instances only because they made that choice.
</Aside>

### Contact details in the jobs and disability domains

Finding a match tells you that a relevant blue dot **exists**. It does not show phone numbers.

In these domains, personal contact details are released only once both parties agree to connect. The domain's `network.json` enforces this (the code calls a domain a *network*): each action interaction declares `reveals_pii_on_status`, the statuses at which personal details become visible. In the reference `blue_dot` domain that status is `accepted`, on every interaction. The Purple Dot domain example uses the same rule.

The Orange Dot (tourism) domain is the exception. Its listings have no actions, and the listed phone number is public on purpose so visitors can call.

## How a signal moves between instances

1. **Create.** A signal is created (directly, by voice, or by an aggregator) and stored as an item in an instance.
2. **Discover.** The instance uses *count-first discovery*: it asks peer instances how many relevant items they hold, picks only the relevant peers, and fetches just the items it needs.
3. **Match.** Candidate items are merged and scored, and the best matches are listed.
4. **Act.** An **action** between two items (for example `apply` on Blue or `connect` on Yellow) produces an **event** that records the result.

Because each instance fetches only what it needs, discovery keeps working as more instances join, and no instance has to query every other one. The details are in [Read & Write Paths](/core-concepts/technical/read-write-paths/).

## Where signals come from

- **Direct.** A person or organisation creates a blue dot through a web or voice interface.
- **Voice AI.** Signals are collected by phone call, in local languages, at a fraction of the cost of a field survey (see the [pilot figures](/explore/pilots/)).
- **Aggregators.** Organisations create signals in bulk for the participants they sign up, using the [Aggregator DPG](/core-concepts/aggregators/). Four intake methods are built in: offline camp, bulk upload, outbound campaign and inbound QR campaign.

For every step from deciding who to bring in to a live, verified blue dot, see [The Blue Dot Lifecycle](/core-concepts/blue-dot-lifecycle/).
