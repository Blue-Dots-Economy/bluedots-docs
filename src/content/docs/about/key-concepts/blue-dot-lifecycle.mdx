Every participant goes through the **same seven steps** to become a blue dot: a live, verified listing that people and organisations nearby can find. A participant is anyone who lists something: someone who needs help, someone who offers it, or a support service.

Each step is done either **by an organisation** (an adaptor or aggregator, acting on the participant's behalf) or **by the participant themselves**. Every step runs on software that already exists, so an adaptor configures it and builds nothing new.

:::note[Two lifecycles, don't confuse them]
This page describes how a person or organisation becomes a blue dot. How a stored item is then found, matched and acted on is a separate process, covered in [From listing to match](/about/the-blue-dots-approach/#from-listing-to-match) and [Read & Write Paths](/get-started/architecture/read-write-paths/).
:::

## Who the participants are

Each domain defines its own participant types. The steps below are the same for all of them.

| Domain | Someone who needs something | Someone who offers something |
|---|---|---|
| Blue Dot (jobs) | `seeker`: a young person looking for work | `provider`: an employer with a vacancy. The deployed pilot schemas also have `service_provider`: an organisation offering training, counselling or placement help. |
| Yellow Dot (education) | `student`: a student looking for tutoring or career counselling | A tutor or counsellor |
| Purple Dot (persons with disabilities) | `seeker`: a person with a disability looking for devices, services or work | `provider`: an organisation or individual offering them |
| Orange Dot (tourism) | None. Visitors browse the listings. | `practitioner`: a homestay owner, artisan or guide. Listing only, with no requests to send or accept. |

The full list of domains and their status is on [Domains, Participant Types & Instances](/about/key-concepts/networks-domains-instances/#the-domains-today).

## Who does what

| Step | Done by | Where it happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Decide who to bring in | Adaptor | Off the platform: a planning decision |
| 2. Choose the route in | Adaptor or aggregator | A setting, per campaign or area |
| 3. Data collection | Aggregator, on the participant's behalf | Aggregator DPG |
| 4. Completing the profile | The participant | AI Voice Agent or web app → Signals DPG |
| 5. Verification checks | The system | Signals DPG |
| 6. Verified | Adaptor or aggregator: **a person** | Aggregator DPG |
| 7. Live | The system | Signals DPG (and the voice bot) |

---

## 1. Decide who to bring in

This step applies to every participant type, in every domain. Examples: a young person looking for work, a student looking for a tutor, a person with a disability who needs a wheelchair, or a homestay owner who wants visitors.

The work here is choosing who to bring in, and on what criteria. The adaptor chooses the group, then picks the **aggregator**: the local organisation with reach and trust on the ground. Examples are a skilling centre, a school, a disability organisation or a tourism body.

See [Aggregators](/about/key-concepts/aggregators/) for who can be one, and [Activating Blue Dots in a District](/about/district-activation/) for how a district picks its first use case.

## 2. Choose the route in

The adaptor or aggregator chooses how a group will be signed up:

- **Assisted.** The adaptor or a field team brings the participant in on their behalf.
- **Self.** The participant starts the process on their own.

Both routes are built in. The choice is a setting, made per campaign or area.

In the Aggregator DPG this is a per-link `registration_mode`, declared under `aggregator.registration_modes` in `aggregator.config.yaml`. Each mode maps to a submission shape: `account_only` (create the account now, complete the profile later) or `account_and_profile` (capture everything in one pass). The database column is only a key, and the config is the single source of truth.

## 3. Data collection (assisted route)

On the assisted route, the aggregator collects participant data with whichever method fits. All four are built into the Aggregator DPG.

| Intake method | What it looks like | What it uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Offline camp** | Data collected in person, for example at a school, a camp or a community event | Aggregator portal, assisted entry |
| **Bulk upload** | An existing list (CSV, database) imported in one go | CSV upload → background worker → bulk-create into Signals |
| **Outbound campaign** | The aggregator contacts people by email or a voice bot | Notifications + voice DPG |
| **Inbound campaign** | The participant scans a QR code shared by the aggregator and starts on their own | Shareable registration links, with roll-up metrics |

## 4. Completing the profile

Whether the participant came through an aggregator or on their own, they complete the profile through one of two channels:

- **AI Voice Agent (primary).** A phone call, for participants who prefer speaking to typing. Its prompts are published openly. See [AI Voice Agent Prompts](/deploy/agents/voice-agent-prompts/).
- **Web app (fallback).** A self-serve web app built on the Signals DPG. Each domain shows it with its own name and colours.

Both channels write into the **same profile**, stored by the Signals DPG. The participant types for each domain are already defined in its schema. See [Participant Profiles](/about/key-concepts/participant-profiles/).

## 5. Verification checks

Before a profile can go live, three checks are applied:

1. **Structured.** The data is in the shape the schema expects.
2. **Completed.** All required fields are filled in.
3. **Consented.** Consent has been recorded.

The system runs these checks automatically. They run against the [item schema](/get-started/architecture/schema-driven-model/), so changing what "structured" and "completed" mean is a schema edit with no code change. Each participant type lists the checks it needs in `go_live_required`. Orange Dot listings are public, so the tourism domain does not require the consent check.

## 6. Verified

A person from the adaptor or the aggregator reviews the profile and confirms it is genuine and ready to go live.

:::tip[The one step a person does by hand]
Software runs the checks, and **a person confirms the result.** This is the only step that needs a person, on purpose: it is how the aggregator's local knowledge vouches for the blue dot.
:::

The review happens in the Aggregator DPG. A coordinator from the aggregator, or a person from the adaptor, checks the fields that can be verified and then marks the profile as verified.

## 7. Live

The blue dot is now live. Others can see it and it can be matched. For example, nearby employers can find a job seeker in the jobs domain, and tutors can find a student in the Yellow Dot domain.

From here, two capabilities are available in the Signals DPG and the voice bot:

- **Discover.** Finding, and being found by, relevant matches.
- **Connect.** Contacting a match, or being contacted by one.

Orange Dot (tourism) listings have Discover only. Visitors find a listing and call the number shown.

:::caution[Contact details are not public]
**In the jobs and disability domains, personal contact details are shared only once both parties agree to connect.** A match shows that a relevant blue dot exists. It does not show phone numbers. See [Consent and contact details](/about/key-concepts/signals/#consent-and-contact-details).
:::

## The lifecycle in practice

These seven steps were used in the jobs pilots in Ghaziabad and Dharwad, with one group of job seekers and one group of employers, each using a different intake method. See [The lifecycle in practice](/explore/pilots/#the-lifecycle-in-practice) on the Pilots page for both, step by step.
