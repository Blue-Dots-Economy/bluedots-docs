Blue Dots helps people and organisations near each other find each other. Each sector runs as its own domain with a colour: Blue for jobs, Yellow for education, Purple for persons with disabilities, and so on. All the domains run on one set of open-source software.

Every domain works the same way. A person creates a blue dot by phone call or in the app, in their own language, in [2–3 minutes](/about/the-blue-dots-approach/). A local organisation checks it. People nearby who match find it. In the jobs and disability domains, contact details are shared only after both sides agree. Orange (tourism) listings are public on purpose.

## Which domains exist today

| Domain | Need | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Blue | Jobs for young people | Live, piloted |
| Yellow | Education | Domain defined |
| Purple | Persons with disabilities | Domain defined |
| Orange | Tourism | Domain defined |
| Pink | Work for women | Possible |
| Green | Farming | Possible |
| Brown | Small businesses | Possible |

"Domain defined" means the domain has a published **minimum standard**: the list of fields every blue dot in that domain must have, so any organisation that uses it can share and find blue dots with others. The software can run any domain that has one. "Possible" means the idea is described but no minimum standard exists yet. For each domain's participant types and details, see [Domains, Participant Types & Instances](/about/key-concepts/networks-domains-instances/#the-domains-today).

### <img class="dot-icon" src="/dots/dot-blue.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true" />Blue Dot — Livelihoods for Youth

Status: **Live, piloted.** Young people and employers often don't know about each other even two bus stops apart. When they miss each other, young people lose income and employers stay short of staff.

Example: a young person registers by phone call in their language, and a skilling centre verifies the profile. A nearby employer with a vacancy sees it and sends an interview invite. The young person accepts before their number is shared. See [Pilots](/explore/pilots/) for results.

See the [Blue Dot (jobs) minimum standard](https://github.com/Blue-Dots-Economy/signals-dpg/blob/main/examples/schemas/blue_dot/network.json).

### <img class="dot-icon" src="/dots/dot-yellow.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true" />Yellow Dot — Education Avenues and Services

Status: **Domain defined.** The Yellow Dot (education) domain connects students with tutors and counsellors near them.

For example, a Class 12 commerce student asks for help with accounts, after a school or NGO helps them sign up. A tutor who teaches Class 12 commerce and will travel up to 5 km sends a connect request. The student accepts.

See the [Yellow Dot minimum standard](https://github.com/Blue-Dots-Economy/signals-dpg/blob/main/examples/schemas/yellow_dot/network.json).

### <img class="dot-icon" src="/dots/dot-purple.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true" />Purple Dot — Economic Participation by Persons with Disabilities

Status: **Domain defined.** For persons with disabilities (PwDs), not finding work and services often means leaving the economy. Every month spent waiting for an assistive device, skilling or a livelihood option is lost income. The Purple Dot domain lets livelihoods, schemes and services find PwDs in the same city.

For example, a person with a locomotor disability asks for a wheelchair and for help finding work. A disabled persons' organisation (DPO) helps them register. Providers in the same city who serve that disability see the request, such as an NGO that gives devices and a training centre. Either side sends a connect request, and the other accepts.

In February 2026, the needs of over **one lakh (100,000) persons with disabilities**, across livelihoods, schemes and services, were collected across multiple districts in ten days at about ₹10 per interaction, against ₹500+ for a traditional field survey.

See the [Purple Dot minimum standard](https://github.com/Blue-Dots-Economy/signals-dpg/blob/main/examples/schemas/purple_dot/network.json).

### <img class="dot-icon" src="/dots/dot-orange.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true" />Orange Dot — Growing the Tourism Economy

Status: **Domain defined.** Local guides, tour experiences and artisans are often not listed anywhere online. Travellers who can't find them go to crowded hubs, and local entrepreneurs lose business.

For example, a homestay owner or a folk artist lists what they offer, with area, languages and price band. A tourism body verifies the listing. Visitors find it in the app and call the number shown. Orange listings and numbers are public on purpose, so no agreement step is needed.

See the [Orange Dot minimum standard](https://github.com/Blue-Dots-Economy/signals-dpg/blob/main/examples/schemas/orange_dot/network.json).

### <img class="dot-icon" src="/dots/dot-pink.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true" />Pink Dot — Greater Female Labour-Force Participation

Status: **Possible.** Women and flexible-work opportunities are often not listed anywhere online. Every day spent looking for work is lost income.

For example, a woman who stitches blouses at home could list her work, with her self-help group (SHG) vouching for her. Nearby boutiques could see it and ask to connect. She would accept before her number is shared.

No minimum standard has been published yet.

### <img class="dot-icon" src="/dots/dot-green.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true" />Green Dot — Boosting Efficiency of Farming

Status: **Possible.** Farm labour, equipment repair and storage are often not listed anywhere online. A farmer who can't find a local mechanic or labour in time loses productivity and margin.

For example, a farmer could ask for a tractor for two days or a soil test. A farmer producer organisation (FPO) could help them sign up. A nearby custom hiring centre or soil-testing lab could see the request. The farmer would accept before contact.

No minimum standard has been published yet.

### <img class="dot-icon" src="/dots/dot-brown.svg" alt="" aria-hidden="true" />Brown Dot — Powering Growth for SMBs

Status: **Possible.** Small businesses that are not listed anywhere online miss nearby customers. They also struggle to find local services and supplies, so they stay small.

For example, a kirana shop could ask for a regular supplier of packaged atta, after a trade association helps it sign up. Nearby wholesalers could see the request. The shop would accept before contact.

No minimum standard has been published yet.

## How a new colour is added

A new colour needs only new data rules; the software stays the same, because Blue Dots is [schema-driven](/get-started/architecture/schema-driven-model/). The steps are:

1. Define the **participant types** for the domain: who asks and who offers.
2. Define the **item schemas**, for example a service-offer type or an eligibility-profile type.
3. Configure the instance's `SERVED_DOMAINS` (the participant types it serves; the code calls them *domains*).
4. Add ways for people to sign up and any domain-specific notifications.
5. Pilot and measure.

For the files and settings to change, follow [Adding a new domain](/deploy/configure/adding-a-domain/).

See [Use Cases](/explore/use-cases/) for an example from each domain.
