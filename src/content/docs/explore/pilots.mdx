Both pilots ran on the Blue Dot (jobs) network, the first network to go live. The pilots in **Ghaziabad** (Uttar Pradesh) and **Dharwad** (Karnataka) tested one question: could employers and job seekers already in the district find each other and act?

The jobs and job seekers the pilots found were already in the district.

## Jobs found that were not listed online

When roughly **10% of MSMEs** in each district were listed, **over 10,000 local job openings were found per district** — many of which had been circulating through word-of-mouth and fragmented postings for months. During the same period, leading national platforms showed **fewer than 100** active listings for the same districts.

The jobs already existed, but almost none of them were listed online.

## Job seekers listed in two weeks

In each of Dharwad and Ghaziabad, **over 15,000 job seekers** were found and listed as dots in just **two weeks** — **20,000+ across the two districts**.

Listing by voice call cut the time to capture what each person needs to **2–3 minutes**, replacing physical camps, job fairs and intermediary networks that previously took weeks of mobilisation.

## Outcomes

- Placement conversion rose from **around 5%** at conventional placement drives and job fairs to **around 50%** in targeted, digitally enabled drives, where both candidates and jobs are listed before the event.
- Time to discover a relevant candidate fell from **more than 2 weeks to under 2 days**.
- Jobs that once required long commutes — often to another city — were now **within 2 bus stops**.

## The lifecycle in practice

Both districts ran the same [seven-step lifecycle](/core-concepts/blue-dot-lifecycle/) — one cohort of job seekers, one of job providers, reaching them by different routes.

### Dharwad — electrician graduates from ITI Dharwad

ITI Dharwad trains electricians every year, but its graduates had no way of being found by employers beyond word of mouth. The institute became the **aggregator** for the batch.

| Step | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1. Decide who to bring in | The district identified ITI Dharwad as the aggregator with direct reach to a graduating batch of electricians, who had real skills but were not listed online |
| 2. Choose the route in | **Assisted** — the institute already held student records |
| 3. Data collection | **Bulk upload** of the whole batch at once, rather than student by student |
| 4. Completing the profile | Each graduate reached by **AI Voice Agent** — a short call, at a fraction of the cost of a manual survey, confirming role preferences, location and trade skills. The webapp stayed available for students who preferred it |
| 5. Verification checks | Each profile checked automatically to confirm it was in the right format, complete and consented to |
| 6. Verified | ITI Dharwad reviewed and confirmed the batch on the Aggregator DPG |
| 7. Live | Each electrician listed as a live job seeker dot — local manufacturers could find exactly this skill set for the first time, instead of relying on informal referrals |

### Ghaziabad — an MSME association turning job openings into dots

Ghaziabad's manufacturing MSMEs had jobs to fill, often circulating only by word of mouth for months. A local MSME association took on the **aggregator** role for its member businesses.

| Step | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1. Decide who to bring in | The Ghaziabad administration, with UPSDM and the DIC, identified the MSME association as best placed to reach its manufacturing members |
| 2. Choose the route in | **Assisted** — an onboarding drive at a member gathering, since many owners would not have registered on their own |
| 3. Data collection | An **inbound campaign** — members scanned a shared QR code and registered their open roles on the spot |
| 4. Completing the profile | Each business completed its listing — role, requirements, location — through the AI Voice Agent or the webapp |
| 5. Verification checks | Each listing checked automatically to confirm it was in the right format, complete and consented to |
| 6. Verified | The association reviewed and confirmed new listings on the Aggregator DPG |
| 7. Live | Each opening a live dot on the Blue Dot network |

Across the district, this conversion took the pilot from **under 100 jobs visible on national platforms to over 10,000 local openings** found from under 10% of MSMEs — with discovery time falling from over two weeks to under two days.

## Named proof

> Shri Abhinav Gopal, IAS, Chief Development Officer, Ghaziabad, outlined how job and candidate aggregators come together to list jobs and job seekers as dots across the district through a **Jobs Facilitation Centre (JFC)**.

In **Dharwad**, manufacturers were convened by a local MSME association to turn their job openings into dots, and electrician graduates from **ITI Dharwad** could be found online.

## Ecosystem uptake

A range of organisations are already using Blue Dots — placement and skilling organisations sharpening their targeting, assessment providers, and local operators scaling deployment. Named participants from the pilots include:

- Head Held High
- Recex
- JobsUFL
- TRRAIN
- Proof Of Skill
- Digital Labour Chowk

## Momentum

Both districts are expanding the approach internally. **Uttar Pradesh and Karnataka are scaling it to multiple districts across each state in the coming year.**
