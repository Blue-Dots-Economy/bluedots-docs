Better local discovery can add roughly **₹87,500 crore** of annual economic activity across India's most progressive districts. The figure counts only people who find work through the Blue Dot (jobs) domain; other domains are not included. This page shows the full derivation behind that number.

## Core logic

Better local discovery addresses the [paradox of proximity](/overview/paradox-of-proximity/) by adding workers who were always present but not listed anywhere online. Once employers can find them, they **earn and spend locally**, and that spending creates further activity through a **local multiplier**.

The model is built bottom-up from a single base district — **Ghaziabad** — and then scaled to **India's top 100 progressive districts**.

## Base: Ghaziabad

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| District population (2011) | ~47 lakh (4.7 million): 24 lakh men, 23 lakh women |
| Latent workforce — Youth (18–29) | ~9.2 lakh (920,000) |
| Latent workforce — Women (30–45) | ~4.5 lakh (450,000) |
| Latent workforce — Persons with disabilities (18–45) | ~40,000 (2.2% prevalence) |

These groups live in the district, but most formal employers cannot find them.

## Step 1 — New workforce participation

A conservative **5%** of each group finds work through better local discovery:

| Group | Population | 5% added to workforce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Youth (18–29) | ~9.2 lakh | ~46,000 |
| Women (30–45) | ~4.5 lakh | ~22,000 |
| Persons with disabilities (18–45) | ~40,000 | ~2,000 |
| **Total** | | **~70,000** |

## Step 2 — Local spending

Lower-income and first-time earners retain a high share of income locally. Per-worker annual spend distribution (illustrative, on a ~₹1.5 lakh income):

| Sector | Typical distribution | Local spend (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food & retail | 35% | 52,500 |
| Housing/rent | effective ~4% (stays at home; 20% × 20% utilities) | 6,000 |
| Transport | 10% | 15,000 |
| Discretionary | 20% | 30,000 |
| Savings | 15% | 0 (not counted as local activity) |
| **Total spends** | | **1,03,500** |

Local spending per worker ≈ **₹1,00,000/year** (≈67% of income after excluding savings).

**GDP addition to Ghaziabad from local spending:**

> 70,000 workers × ₹1 lakh = **₹700 crore**

## Step 3 — Multiplier effect

Each ₹1 of local spending generates ~₹1.5 of local economic activity (retail, housing, transport, education, services).

**GDP addition from the multiplier:**

> ₹700 crore × 1.5 = **₹1,050 crore** for Ghaziabad

## Scaling to the top 100 districts

Ghaziabad's population is ~47 lakh. India's top 100 districts (excluding metros) have a total population of ~25 crore (250 million), giving a conservative scaling factor of **50×**.

| Component | Ghaziabad (₹ crore/yr) | Top 100 districts (₹ crore/yr) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local spending | ~700 | ~35,000 |
| Multiplier on local spend (×1.5) | ~1,050 | ~52,500 |
| **Total** | | **~87,500 crore** |

:::caution[Caveats]
Districts with fewer MSMEs (micro, small and medium enterprises) or weaker local business links may generate less; stronger cluster districts more — **Ghaziabad is a reasonable mid-point**. The estimate also excludes compounding from repeat local-spending cycles.
:::

## What this calculation excludes

The headline figure is deliberately conservative. It leaves out three additive channels:

1. **Welfare & scheme utilisation** — faster discovery lifts utilisation for a *different* population, so the effect adds to the headline figure without counting anyone twice.
2. **Services activation** — visible local providers (teachers, repair, care, tourism, agri-services) raise service-consumption GDP.
3. **MSME productivity & revenue** — faster hiring and customer discovery improves output.

Including these would raise the figure materially — so **₹87,500 crore is a conservative estimate**.

## Research basis

| Source | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Moretti (2010, *American Economic Review*) | One tradable-sector job creates ~1.6 additional local service jobs. |
| Bartik (2019, Upjohn Institute) | Multiplier of 1.5 on a conservative restatement. |
| India Policy Forum — Chaurey & Nayyar (2022) | A 10% increase in tradable employment produces ~4.2% increase in non-tradable employment and 2.8% increase in local firms. |
| Jena (2017) | MSME clusters in 21 states produce 96.3% of India's MSME manufacturing output. |

**Local-spending retention (67%):** the marginal propensity to consume is 0.6–0.9 in Indian research; tier-2 retention is higher than metros due to lower e-commerce and financial leakage.

*Population and workforce figures: Census of India 2011 (Ghaziabad); PLFS, MoSPI 2024.*
